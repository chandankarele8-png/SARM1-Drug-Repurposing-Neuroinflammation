# Computational Drug Repurposing Targeting SARM1 in Neuroinflammation

## Project Overview

This project explores computational approaches for identifying potential drug candidates targeting Sterile Alpha and TIR Motif Containing 1 (SARM1), a key regulator of neuronal degeneration and neuroinflammatory processes.

SARM1 possesses NADase activity through its TIR domain and plays an important role in neuronal injury and axonal degeneration. Modulating SARM1 activity represents a promising research direction for investigating potential therapeutic strategies for neurodegenerative conditions.

## Objectives

- Investigate the structural and functional characteristics of SARM1.
- Identify potential drug candidates through drug repurposing approaches.
- Analyze protein–ligand interactions and binding affinities.
- Investigate interactions involving the SARM1 TIR domain.
- Evaluate candidate compounds using computational structural biology tools.

## Tools and Databases

- **NCBI:** Biological sequence information.
- **RCSB PDB:** Protein structural data.
- **PubChem / DrugBank:** Chemical and drug information.
- **DrugRep:** Drug repurposing analysis.
- **PyRx:** Molecular docking.
- **PyMOL:** Protein structure visualization.
- **Discovery Studio Visualizer:** Protein–ligand interaction analysis.

## Methodology

1. Target protein identification and structural analysis.
2. Retrieval and preparation of protein and ligand structures.
3. Drug candidate selection and preparation.
4. Molecular docking using PyRx.
5. Binding interaction analysis.
6. Visualization of protein–ligand complexes.
7. Comparative evaluation of docking results.

## Research Significance

This computational study aims to explore potential SARM1-targeting compounds and provide a foundation for further experimental investigation into neuroinflammation and neurodegeneration.

**Note:** Computational predictions require experimental validation before any therapeutic conclusions can be made.
